# Foundational Philosophy

> Source: §1.2 of the Mokshly YouSourceful Platform Design Document.

Mokshly's design is grounded in **two foundational truths**. Together, they shape every feature decision in the product.

---

## The First Truth — The Practice Is a Doorway, Not a Destination

Mokshly is not a regime of seven daily practices to complete. It is a system for cultivating Awareness. The practices are doorways through which Awareness becomes available.

- Some days you walk through one doorway.
- Some days three.
- Some days none — and still the Awareness is with you.

We do **not** celebrate completion. We honor consistency and intention. We do not measure success by boxes checked, but by **how differently you meet your life**.

### Product consequences

This truth has consequences throughout the product:

- It shapes how we compute the **MCI** — consistency of showing up, not variety of practices.
- It shapes what we display on the **Today screen** — one practice, not a checklist of seven.
- It shapes our **nudge engine** — we never nudge about practices skipped today.
- It shapes our **cohort prompts** — several explicitly invite reflection on when formal practice was absent yet Awareness was present.

The evidence of practice is in the quality of a person's living — how they breathe through a difficult conversation, how they pause before reacting, how they attend to what matters. These are invisible to any tracker. They are the only things that matter.

---

## The Second Truth — The Player Keeps Their Own Score

Mokshly's architecture draws inspiration from golf as a philosophy of practice. **Six truths from golf** shape our design:

1. **The player keeps their own score.** No one else tracks your practice. You log what you did. You alone see your measure of consistency.
2. **The consistency measure is sacred and private.** Your MCI is your own. Nobody — not your club, not your playing partners, not your insurance — sees it.
3. **You compete only with yourself.** Every practitioner is on their own arc, never compared to another.
4. **The club provides the course, not the supervision.** Your employer, your membership organization, or the Mokshly Commons provides the platform. They do not watch you play.
5. **You choose when and how to play.** Guided sessions and self-logged sessions count equally.
6. **Return is always welcomed.** Missing a week doesn't end your practice. The course is there next week.

### A note on the metaphor

This philosophy is **internal to the design team**. It shapes how we build the system. It **never surfaces as golf metaphors in user-facing language**. Mokshly speaks in its own calm, minimal voice. The golf philosophy lives behind that voice, informing the architecture without intruding on the experience.
