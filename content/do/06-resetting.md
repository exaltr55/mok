# Practice 6 — I M Resetting

Full Script — Narrator Voiceover  

PART A — Learning the Practice  

[Narrator voiceover. Calm, warm, unhurried tone. Paired with gentle visuals — quiet domestic scenes, hands resting, a phone face-down, a simple meal, natural light.]  

In the practices we have explored so far,  
we have worked with the breath,  
the mind, with talking, with writing,  
and with conscious movement of the body.  

Each of these practices helped us build Awareness —  
Awareness in motion, Awareness in expression,  
Awareness in the body.  

Now we turn to something equally important,  
and often quietly overlooked.  

The ability to stop.  

This practice is called I M Resetting.  

And at its heart,  
it is simply about giving ourselves permission to pause.  

To step away — even briefly —  
from constant stimulation. From habits  
that run on their own. From patterns  
and compulsions  
that shape our days without us quite noticing.  

Just as we recharge our devices  
when they run low,  
our bodies  
and minds also need regular moments of restoration —  
to return to balance, to clarity,  
and to a genuine sense of choice in how we live.  

Resetting works through two simple forms.  

## Dietary Resetting

A temporary,  
gentle pause from food  
that allows the body to rest,  
repair, and rebalance.  

## Digital Resetting

A pause from screens  
and constant incoming information  
that allows the nervous system  
and the mind to settle and recover.  

Each of these can be practiced at two levels.  

## Daily Mini-Reset

Short, gentle pauses  
that fit naturally into everyday life.  

It acts like a tune-up —  
preventing overload from accumulating.  

## Weekly Deeper Reset

A longer pause  
that allows for a more complete restoration of body and mind.  

It acts like a reboot —  
clearing accumulated fatigue  
and restoring a sense of equilibrium.  

When practiced regularly,  
I M Resetting brings real  
and noticeable benefits.  

Physically,  
the body gets time to repair  
and renew. Digestion improves.  
Sleep deepens. Energy becomes more stable.  

Mentally, the mind grows clearer.  
Mental fog begins to lift.  
Focus improves. Attention feels lighter  
and more spacious.  

Emotionally,  
there is less reactivity. Less comparison.  
Less background stress.  
More calm. More presence.  

And perhaps most importantly,  
resetting gradually restores a sense of freedom —  
freedom from compulsion,  
freedom from automatic habit.  

We begin to notice an urge without immediately obeying it.  

We begin to respond consciously,  
rather than react automatically.  

That shift — small as it may seem —  
changes everything.  

At the heart of this practice lies an ancient yogic principle called *Pratyahara* —  
the withdrawal of the senses.  

Not suppression. Not denial.  

Simply stepping back —  
so that we are no longer quietly controlled by cravings,  
habits, or the relentless pull of stimulation.  

Resetting is not a punishment.  

It is a practice of freedom.  

You may begin with daily resets  
and explore weekly ones when you feel ready.  

There is no rush. There is no forcing.  

Only conscious pauses —  
taken gently, practiced with care,  
and returned to again and again.  

PART B — Practicing I M Resetting  

Alright — let's talk about how Resetting actually works in your day.  

There are two flavors:  

A **daily mini-reset** —  
gentle, brief, every-day kind of thing.  

And a **weekly deeper reset** —  
longer, more complete, properly restorative.  

Each comes in two forms: through food, and through screens.  
(Yes, your phone is invited to this party. Sorry, phone.)  

You don't need to start with all of them.  
Pick the one that feels least scary.  
Build from there.  

Consistency beats intensity. Every time.  

And remember — these aren't really about rest.  
They're about *noticing*.  

When you reach for food.  
When you reach for your phone.  
What's actually driving that hand?  

With a bit of Awareness,  
patterns that felt automatic start to loosen on their own.  
What no longer serves you slowly falls away —  
not through willpower,  
but through quiet, steady noticing.  

## Dietary Resetting

A dietary reset is not only about food.  

It is about Awareness.  

It brings gentle attention to *what* you eat,  
*how* you eat, and *when* you eat.  

Sometimes it includes giving the digestive system  
a genuine break — letting the body shift  
from constant processing into something quieter.  
Repair. Restoration. Rebalancing.  

### Before you begin  

Set yourself up to win.  

Plan meals ahead. Don't leave snack-traps lying around  
(you know which ones). Keep water and herbal teas ready.  
Tell the people you live with — if that helps.  

Set a tiny intention. Something like:  

*"Today, I'm giving my body a little space to reset."*  

And — important — if you have any medical condition  
or specific dietary needs, please talk to your doctor first.  
We're not going to play hero here.  

### Daily 3-Hour Dietary Reset

This is the easiest place to start.  
Honestly — most people are halfway here already.  

If you usually sleep around 10 pm,  
wrap up dinner by around 7. Then — nothing else.  
No late-night snacks. No desserts. No "just a little something."  
Water and herbal teas are welcome.  

That's it. That's the whole thing.  

Even this short pause, taken with attention,  
helps digestion, deepens sleep,  
and lets you wake up a little lighter, a little clearer.  

(You can also choose any 3-hour window during the day —  
but evenings work especially well, since the body is already winding down.)  

### Weekly 20-Hour Dietary Reset

This is the deeper one.  
A longer pause that gives the body real space to restore.  

A quick honest note: if you have, or have had, a difficult relationship with food —  
or any history of an eating-related condition — please skip this section,  
or check with a healthcare provider first. This reset is meant to feel light,  
not pressured.  

Pick a day that fits your life.  

A structure that works for most:  

Stop eating around 7 to 8 pm on Friday.  
Carry that through Saturday morning and into the early afternoon.  
Break the reset around 4 pm with something light and wholesome.  

In between — water and herbal teas, as much as you like.  

If this is new, or the pause feels tough,  
ease yourself in with very light food —  
simple fruit, a light soup, boiled potatoes,  
or kichri (rice and lentils — a classic for a reason).  

This isn't about pushing through. Don't be a hero.  

It's about *listening* — to what the body actually needs,  
versus what it just *happens* to crave.  

Staying With the Practice  

In the beginning,  
you may notice hunger at familiar times of day.  

These are often what we call hunger-clock signals —  
habit patterns, not true hunger.  

They usually pass within ten to fifteen minutes.  

This is where the practice deepens.  

You begin to see  
that not every urge is a real need.  

Some are patterns. Some are old conditioning.  

And simply by noticing them —  
without immediately acting —  
you begin to gently change them.  

When cravings arise, try quietly reframing:  

"I am choosing freedom, not compulsion."  

You can also support yourself by:  

drinking a glass of water,  
taking a short mindful walk,  
or writing a few lines in your journal.  

Your I M Writing practice can be a powerful companion here —  
helping you stay connected to your intention  
when the pull of habit feels strong.  

Resetting is not deprivation.  
It is giving yourself a holiday from compulsive habits.  

Like simple, gentle housekeeping —  
it clears out the old  
and creates space for something fresher to arrive.  

## Digital Resetting

A digital reset gives the nervous system a break  
from the *constant* drip of stimulation.  

The mind slows down. Settles.  
Becomes genuinely clearer.  
(Yes — there's a "you" under all those notifications.)  

### Before you begin  

Set yourself up.  

Tell friends, family, colleagues.  
Decide in advance how you'll use the time.  
Silence notifications. Put devices out of reach —  
out of sight is usually out of mind.  

Focus Mode or Do Not Disturb on your phone  
lets messages pause without you having to white-knuckle it.  

### Daily 3-Hour Digital Reset

Pick a window — ideally 7 pm until sleep.  

During that time:  
TVs off. Laptops closed. Phones away.  
Putting the phone in another room helps more than you'd think.  
(Yes, even out of arm's reach. Trust us.)  

Fill the window with something else:  
conversation, journaling, reading,  
a creative hobby, a non-digital game —  
sudoku on paper, a puzzle, a quick sketch.  

The mind starts to recognize this window as a signal —  
*the day is winding down. Time to come home.*  

### Weekly 20-Hour Digital Reset

Pick a longer window.  

You can pair this with your dietary reset if you like —  
Friday 8 pm to Saturday 4 pm,  
or Saturday evening to Sunday afternoon.  

If twenty hours feels intimidating, start smaller —  
four waking hours, then gradually stretch.  

No failure here. Only steps. Even half a reset is a real reset.  

**During the reset**  
Devices off, or airplane mode for emergencies only.  

Fill the time with things that actually feed you:  
journaling, mindful walks, cooking with attention,  
meditation, simple rest, a creative hobby,  
meeting a friend somewhere unhurried.  

This isn't empty time.  
It's restorative time. And it tends to feel more *alive*  
than you expected.  

**Returning to screens**  
Come back slowly. Start with something uplifting —  
not the news, not the doom-scroll, not the inbox.  

Keep the rest of the day light on digital consumption.  
The clarity you just earned — protect it for a bit.  

Staying With the Practice  

You may notice what we might call the phantom phone reach.  

The hand moves almost automatically —  
before the mind has even decided.  

When it happens:  

Pause. Smile gently. Notice it.  

That moment of noticing is the practice working.  

Each time you catch the impulse  
and pause before acting,  
you are gently retraining the mind.  

Old patterns begin to loosen.  
Space begins to open.  

And with that space,  
comes something valuable —  
genuine choice.  

A small thing that helps:  

Keep the phone out of sight.  
Place it in a drawer.  
Or add one small,  
simple barrier —  
even putting it in another room is often enough to interrupt the automatic reach  
and create a moment of conscious choice.  

## Reflecting During Resetting

Journaling can deepen Awareness during both resets.  

You may use these simple prompts as gentle check-ins throughout the day:  

In the morning:  

"What cravings  
or impulses am I noticing?  
How do I usually respond to them?"  

At midday:  

"Without food or screens,  
what thoughts or feelings are surfacing?"  

In the afternoon:  

"What feels lighter right now?  
What clarity is beginning to emerge?"  

There are no right answers.  

Only noticing. Only returning.  
Only the quiet practice of seeing clearly.  

## A Few Helpful Notes

Dietary resetting is not recommended for those who are pregnant  
or breastfeeding,  
or for those recovering from illness.  

Expect cravings and some restlessness —  
especially at the beginning.  

They will pass.  

Use each urge as a gentle reminder:  

"I am choosing freedom over compulsion."  

Resetting works best when it is gentle,  
regular, and honestly adapted to your life.  

You may also adjust this practice with guidance from a healthcare provider  
or Ayurveda practitioner if  
that feels right for you.

By pausing from food and from screens,  

we create space within.  

In that space,  
we begin to see our patterns more clearly —  
the ones that serve us,  
and the ones that no longer do.  

What no longer serves us starts,  
gently, to fall away.  

Not through force. Not through effort.  

Simply through the light of quiet Awareness.  

The body restores. The mind clears.  
The nervous system settles.  

And we return —  
to our food, to our screens, to our lives —  

with greater clarity, a little more balance,  
and a gentleness toward ourselves  
that grows with every conscious pause.  

This is the practice of resetting.  

Simple. Gentle. And quietly powerful.  

Return to it often.
