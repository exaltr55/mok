# Practice 7 — I M Aligning

Practice 7: I M Aligning  

PART A — Learning the Practice  

Through the practices  

you have explored so far,  

you have been returning —  

again and again —  

to awareness, the space or silence in which all things arise and eventually dissolves,  

like waves in the ocean.  

With the breath.  

With the mind.  

With your inner dialogue.  

With your body.  

With your habits.  

Each practice offered  

a simple invitation —  

to pause,  

to notice,  

and to create a little more space  

in how you live.  

Now, in this final practice,  

I M Aligning,  

we bring everything together.  

Alignment is about  

living in greater harmony —  

with who you are,  

with how life is unfolding around you,  

and with what each phase  

of work and life  

is asking of you right now.  

It is not about becoming  

someone different.  

It is not about adding  

more to your plate.  

It is about understanding yourself  

more clearly —  

and making small, conscious choices  

that support that understanding.  

To understand yourself  

more clearly,  

it helps to begin  

with a simple recognition —  

each of us is born  

with a particular nature.  

A set of tendencies,  

rhythms,  

and ways of operating  

that are uniquely our own.  

We see this everywhere,  

once we start to look.  

For example, some people do their sharpest thinking  

in the early morning.  

Others come alive mid-afternoon.  

Some work best  

in long, uninterrupted stretches.  

Others in short, focused sprints.  

Some recover through solitude.  

Others through conversation.  

None of these is better or worse.  

They are simply different —  

expressions of the particular way  

each of us is made.  

And consistently working  

against your own nature  

has a cost  

that accumulates quietly over time.  

Rather than labelling yourself  

a fixed type,  

simply begin to observe.  

At different times,  

you may notice:  

A sense of restlessness —  

scattered, too many inputs.  

A sense of intensity —  

high drive, running close to the edge.  

A sense of heaviness —  

energy low, motivation harder to find.  

These are not problems.  

They are signals —  

your system communicating  

what is present,  

and what might need  

a little adjustment.  

The practice of alignment begins  

with learning to read  

these signals in yourself —  

and responding with care,  

rather than force.  

In the previous practice,  

you learned how to pause —  

to step out of automatic patterns  

and create space.  

That space is where  

alignment begins.  

In this practice,  

you begin to use that space  

to make more conscious choices —  

choices that are better suited  

to your nature,  

your current energy,  

and the life  

you are actually living.  

Not perfect choices.  

Just more aligned ones.  

When you begin to live this way,  

things shift —  

quietly but meaningfully.  

Effort becomes more effective.  

Work feels less like friction,  

and more like flow.  

Relationships feel less strained.  

Life does not become perfect.  

It begins to feel  

more coherent,  

more steady,  

and more easeful.  

Alignment is not control.  

It is not self-improvement  

in the conventional sense.  

It is responsiveness —  

your growing ability  

to notice what is present,  

recognize what is changing,  

and adjust with awareness and care,  

again and again.  

In the next section,  

we bring this into your daily life —  

not as a concept to hold,  

but as something  

you can begin to live.  

PART B — Practicing I M Aligning  

Let’s bring this practice, I M Aligning,  
into your life.  

Not as something to analyze,  
but as something  
you begin to live —  
through small,  
conscious adjustments.  

Alignment does not require  
big changes.  
It grows through simple shifts,  
made consistently.  

At any moment,  
based on your nature  
and what the day is asking of you,  
certain tendencies  
are active within you —  
shaping how you feel,  
how you think,  
and how you respond.  

You may sense restlessness,  
intensity,  
or heaviness.  

These are natural.  
Nothing is wrong.  

Alignment begins  
with simply noticing —  
and then gently  
bringing balance.  

If things feel rushed —  
slow down.  
If things feel intense —  
soften.  

If things feel heavy —  
activate.  

Not forcefully.  
Just enough to return  
toward balance.  

A Simple Daily Rhythm  

Alignment begins  
in small moments  
throughout the day.  

In the morning,  
pause briefly  
before the day begins.  

Notice your body.  
Notice your energy.  
Notice what feels dominant  
right now.  

From that,  
make one small adjustment —  
within what your day allows.  

During the day,  
pause briefly  
and ask:  
Am I in balance right now?  

From there —  
adjust your pace,  
shift your effort,  
or take a short pause.  

One small shift is enough.  

In the evening,  
begin to slow down.  
Reduce stimulation.  
Let your system settle.  

You may reflect gently:  
Did I support myself today?  
Did my choices feel aligned?  

Then let the day close  
without overthinking.  

These small moments,  
practiced consistently,  
quietly build  
your alignment over time.  

The Three Pillars of Alignment  

Alignment becomes real  
when it shows up  
in how you live each day.  

There are three areas  
where this matters most —  
your health,  
your work,  
and your relationships.  

These are the pillars  
that carry your life.  

In each of these,  

you are working with patterns that are already part of you—  

your body,  

your way of functioning,  

and your way of relating.  

Alignment in these areas  
is about honoring  
those patterns —  
while remaining responsive  
to what each phase of life  
is asking of you.  

A Word of Care  

Recognizing  
your inherent pattern  
is not permission  
to be ruled by it.  

The purpose of the earlier practices —  
the breath,  
the mind,  
inner dialogue,  
writing,  
movement,  
and the pause —  
has been to build  
one essential capacity:  

Awareness.  

Awareness allows you  
to notice a tendency  
without being carried by it.  

To see an impulse  
without automatically  
acting on it.  

To recognize a pattern  
without using it  
as an excuse  
for what no longer serves you.  

Without awareness,  
your pattern runs you.  

With awareness,  
you can work with it —  
consciously,  
skillfully,  
and in a way  
that honors who you are  
without becoming  
a prisoner of it.  

So as you explore  
these three pillars,  
hold this gently:  

Your pattern  
is not an identity to defend.  

It is not a reason  
to stay in what is draining  
or unhealthy.  

It is simply a starting point —  
a truth about how you are made,  
to be met with awareness.  

Alignment is not  
rationalizing habits.  

Alignment is choosing  
consciously —  
with a clearer understanding  
of who you are,  
what you need,  
and what this moment  
is truly asking of you.  

As you explore these, remember —  
there are tools available  
to help you understand  
your nature more clearly.  

Use them as companions.  

They help you see  
what supports you,  
what drains you,  
and what each phase of life  
is asking of you.  

1. Aligning Health — Meeting Yourself Where You Are  

You are made  

in a particular way.  

Your body has its own constitution —  

a natural blend  

of elements and tendencies  

that shape  

how you feel,  

how you digest,  

how you move,  

and how you rest.  

Your mind, too,  

has its own rhythms,  

its own sensitivities,  

its own way  

of holding or releasing  

what life brings.  

Together,  

body and mind  

form a living system  

that is uniquely yours.  

Some bodies  

run warm.  

Others run cool.  

Some minds  

move quickly.  

Others with depth  

and quiet deliberation.  

Some people digest food  

easily.  

Others more slowly.  

Some people process emotion  

by talking it out.  

Others by going quiet  

and sitting with it.  

Some feel most alive  

in movement.  

Others in stillness.  

Some thrive  

on variety and change.  

Others on rhythm  

and consistency.  

These patterns  

are not habits  

you have chosen.  

They are part of  

how you are made.  

This is why  

no single routine  

works for everyone.  

What energizes one person  

may exhaust another.  

What nourishes one body  

may unsettle the next.  

What calms one mind  

may feel restless  

to another.  

Alignment in health  

begins here —  

with the honest recognition  

that your body and mind  

each have their own nature,  

their own signals,  

and their own needs.  

Your body and mind  

are also not the same  

from one day to the next.  

Some days they are energized.  

Some days depleted.  

Some days restless.  

Some days slow.  

Your age changes them.  

Your stress changes them.  

The season changes them.  

The phase of life you are in  

changes them.  

Alignment in health  

is not about following  

the right routine.  

It is about meeting yourself  

where you actually are —  

today,  

not yesterday,  

not the version of you  

from ten years ago.  

It is a continuous conversation  

between the nature you were born with,  

and what your body and mind  

are asking of you now.  

Key Alignments  

Align with your mental state, not against it.  

Your mental state shapes  

how you experience everything.  

At times,  

the mind can feel busy,  

restless,  

or overwhelmed.  

At other times,  

it may feel low —  

due to a life situation,  

changes in the body,  

or simply  

from being overextended.  

This is part  

of being human.  

Alignment here  

is not about forcing  

the mind to feel better.  

It is about supporting it —  

with awareness  

and simple actions.  

You can begin  

with small,  

consistent steps:  

Pause and take  

a few steady breaths.  

Create short breaks  

between activities.  

Limit constant input  

from screens and noise.  

Give yourself time  

to rest and reset.  

Write or speak  

what you are processing,  

instead of holding it in.  

Reach out  

to your cohort —  

the others  

walking this path  

alongside you.  

Sometimes  

simply being heard  

by people  

who understand  

the practices you are living  

is enough  

to shift something  

that felt stuck.  

These are simple shifts —  

but they change  

how the mind moves.  

Over time,  

this creates more space,  

more clarity,  

and more steadiness.  

So the mind does not  

drain you —  

it begins to support you.  

Align what you do with the body and mind you have today, not the ones you used to have.  

The exercise that served you  

at twenty or thirty  

may not serve you  

at forty or fifty.  

The late nights  

you once recovered from easily  

may now cost you  

three days.  

The foods that once  

energized you  

may now feel heavy.  

And the way your mind  

used to bounce back quickly  

may now ask  

for more quiet,  

more space,  

more recovery.  

Notice this honestly,  

without resistance.  

Your body and mind  

have not failed you.  

They have changed.  

Alignment means adjusting  

what you do  

to match  

who you are now —  

not pushing  

to keep up  

with who you were.  

Align your rhythm with what your system actually needs.  

Some people  

feel their best  

when life follows  

a predictable rhythm.  

Meals at the same time.  

Sleep at the same hour.  

A steady routine  

that the body and mind  

can rely on.  

For them,  

consistency is not rigidity —  

it is what keeps  

their system stable.  

Other people  

operate differently.  

They can work for long stretches  

without a set schedule,  

eat when it fits,  

sleep when the day allows,  

and still feel steady.  

Their system does not need  

the same structure  

to function well.  

Some fall in the middle of the spectrum.  

No way is better.  

What matters  

is noticing  

which kind of system  

you actually have —  

and honoring it.  

If you are someone  

who needs consistency,  

protect your rhythms.  

Do not let a busy season  

quietly dismantle them.  

If you are someone  

who thrives with flexibility,  

allow that —  

without forcing yourself  

into routines  

that do not fit  

how you operate.  

Alignment here  

is simply choosing a rhythm  

that matches your system,  

not one you think  

you should follow.  

Align your pace with the energy actually available to you.  

Check in, honestly,  

at different points  

in the day.  

Not just physical energy —  

but mental energy too.  

If you are clear and focused,  

use that clarity  

for what matters most.  

If your energy is moderate,  

take on what is steady  

and manageable.  

If your energy is low  

in body or mind —  

do not pretend otherwise.  

Rest if you can, or shift, or simplify.  

Fighting against  

the energy you have  

wastes more than it gains.  

Working with it  

carries you further.  

Align your food with what the moment needs.  

Food is not just fuel.  

It shapes  

how you feel,  

how you think,  

and how you show up  

for the next few hours.  

It affects your body  

and your mind  

in the same meal.  

Before you eat,  

pause and ask:  

What do I need  

right now?  

Lightness,  

so I can stay clear?  

Grounding,  

so I can steady myself?  

Warmth,  

so I can settle?  

Let your choice  

match the answer —  

not habit,  

not convenience,  

not what someone else  

is having.  

Align your recovery with the demands you are carrying.  

Not all days  

are the same.  

A week of heavy work  

asks for more recovery,  

not less.  

A period of travel,  

stress, or change  

asks for more rest,  

not pushing through.  

A time of emotional difficulty  

asks for gentleness —  

not willpower.  

Notice what you are  

actually carrying,  

in body and mind —  

and give yourself  

the recovery  

that matches it.  

This is not indulgence.  

It is alignment between  

what life is asking of you  

and what you give yourself  

to sustain it.  

Closing Reflection on Health  

Your body and mind  

are your closest companions  

through life.  

They change.  

You change.  

Alignment in health  

is not a fixed formula.  

It is a practice  

of meeting yourself  

honestly —  

noticing what you have,  

noticing what you need,  

and choosing accordingly.  

When you do this  

consistently,  

your body and mind  

repay you —  

not with perfection,  

but with steadiness.  

Three Keys for Health  

Know • Honor • Adjust  

Know your body and mind,  

and what they need.  

Honor the habits  

that genuinely fit you.  

Adjust gently  

as your body,  

your season,  

and your life  

continue to change.  

2. Aligning Work — Fitting Your Work to Who You Are  

Work is where  
many of us spend  
most of our time  
and energy.  

When your work  
fits who you are,  
effort feels meaningful.  

When it doesn’t,  
even small tasks  
feel heavy.  

Alignment in work  
is the ongoing practice  
of closing the gap  
between who you are  
and what you do.  

Sometimes this means  
choosing differently  
when you can.  

Sometimes it means  
shaping the work  
you already have  
to better fit your nature.  

Both are acts  
of alignment.  

Some people are natural builders —  

they thrive in creating,  

in taking an idea  

from nothing to something.  

Some are natural organizers —  

they bring order,  

structure,  

and steadiness  

to what already exists.  

Some are natural connectors —  

they work best  

through people,  

relationships,  

and collaboration.  

Some are natural thinkers —  

drawn to ideas,  

analysis,  

and depth.  

Some are natural doers —  

energized by action,  

execution,  

and momentum.  

These are not roles  

you learned.  

They are inclinations  

you were born with —  

a living pattern  

that shapes  

how you engage with work,  

what energizes you,  

and where your contribution  

feels most natural.  

When your work  

aligns with this pattern,  

effort feels meaningful.  

When it doesn't,  

even small tasks  

feel heavy.  

Alignment in work  

is the ongoing practice  

of closing the gap  

between  

who you are  

and what you do.  

Sometimes this means  

choosing differently  

when you can.  

Sometimes it means  

shaping the work  

you already have  

to better fit your nature.  

Both are acts  

of alignment.  

Key Alignments  

Align your work choices with how you actually operate — not how you think you should operate.  

Before taking on  

a new role,  

a project,  

or a major commitment —  

pause,  

and take yourself  

through a few honest questions:  

Does this work  

use the strengths  

that come naturally to me?  

Does it match the pace  

I operate at best?  

Does it suit  

the phase of life  

I am in right now?  

Will I be able  

to sustain this  

over time,  

without losing myself  

in the process?  

You can also take  

simple, practical steps:  

Write out  

what energizes you,  

and what drains you,  

based on the last few months  

of your work.  

Before accepting something new,  

sit with it  

for at least a day —  

not respond in the moment.  

Talk to someone  

who knows both you  

and the opportunity,  

and listen carefully  

to what they notice.  

You will not always  

have a full choice.  

But when you do,  

choose in a way  

that fits  

the person you actually are —  

not the person  

you are trying  

to prove yourself to be.  

Align the work you already have with the parts of you that do it best.  

Most of us  

are not starting fresh.  

We are already inside  

a role,  

a set of responsibilities,  

a body of work.  

Alignment here  

is not about walking away.  

It is about reshaping  

what you already have —  

slowly,  

steadily,  

and realistically.  

You can begin  

with small,  

practical moves:  

Take one week  

to notice  

which parts of your work  

energize you,  

and which parts drain you.  

Make a simple list —  

energizing on one side,  

draining on the other.  

Pick one draining task —  

and ask honestly:  

Can I delegate this?  

Can I simplify this?  

Can I renegotiate this  

with my manager,  

my team,  

or my client?  

Pick one energizing task —  

and ask:  

Can I do more of this?  

Can I lean into it  

when I plan my week?  

Block a portion of your calendar  

for the work  

you do best —  

before it gets filled  

by everything else.  

Alignment here  

is rarely dramatic.  

It is small, steady adjustments  

that, over time,  

reshape your work  

to fit you  

more closely.  

Align your hardest work with your sharpest self.  

You do not bring  

the same clarity  

to every hour of the day.  

Your mind has peaks,  

and it has valleys —  

and working with them  

makes a real difference.  

You can begin by  

getting to know your own rhythm:  

For one week,  

notice when your mind  

feels sharpest.  

Often, it is  

one to three hours  

in the morning.  

Sometimes, mid-afternoon.  

For some,  

it is late evening  

when the world is quiet.  

Once you know your window,  

protect it with intention:  

Schedule your most demanding  

or creative work  

for that window.  

Do not give it  

to routine email,  

low-priority meetings,  

or small tasks.  

Turn off notifications  

during this window  

when you can.  

Save meetings,  

correspondence,  

and execution tasks  

for the steadier but less sharp  

hours.  

When your energy dips,  

do the simpler work —  

or take a genuine break.  

This one alignment,  

practiced consistently,  

often creates  

more real progress  

than working longer hours  

ever will.  

Align your work with the phase of life you are in.  

The work that served you  

at one stage of life  

may not serve you  

at another.  

In your twenties,  

you may have had  

energy to spare.  

In your forties,  

you may be carrying  

more responsibility  

at home.  

In your fifties or sixties,  

you may be looking for  

meaning over intensity.  

Each phase  

asks different things of you.  

Alignment here  

is about noticing  

what this phase needs —  

and responding  

with care,  

not guilt.  

You can do this with  

a few simple practices:  

Once every few months,  

set aside an honest hour  

to ask yourself:  

What is working?  

What is no longer working?  

What does this phase  

of my life need  

that I am not giving it?  

Notice where your time  

is going —  

and whether it matches  

what actually matters  

to you right now.  

If something feels off,  

start small.  

Adjust one commitment.  

Let go of one obligation  

that no longer fits.  

Add one thing  

that supports  

who you are becoming.  

You do not need  

to change everything  

at once.  

But you do deserve work  

that honors the stage  

you are actually in.  

Closing Reflection on Work  

Work will not always  

be easy.  

But when it fits  

who you are —  

at this phase of your life —  

it carries you.  

When it doesn't,  

it wears you down,  

quietly,  

over time.  

Alignment in work  

is the practice  

of closing that gap —  

not all at once,  

but steadily.  

Small adjustments,  

made with awareness,  

that bring your daily work  

closer and closer  

to the person  

you actually are.  

Three Keys for Work  

Choose • Shape • Evolve  

Choose work  

that fits who you are,  

when you have the choice.  

Shape the work  

already in your hands  

around your strengths  

and your rhythms.  

Evolve your work  

as you move through  

the different phases  

of your life.  

3. Aligning Relationships — Matching Connection to Life as It Is  

Relationships are  
 one of the deepest  
 sources of meaning  
 in our lives.  

They are also  
 one of the most common  
 sources of misalignment.  

We change.  
 The other person changes.  
 Life changes around both of us.  

But the shape  
 of our relationships  
 often stays the same —  
 long after it has stopped  
 fitting who we have become.  

Alignment in relationships  
 is the practice  
 of noticing those shifts,  
 and adjusting the connection —  
 so that it continues to support  
 who you are now,  
 and who the other person is now.  

Sometimes this means  
 forming new connections.  
 Sometimes  
 deepening or renegotiating  
 existing ones.  
 And sometimes,  
 gently releasing  
 what no longer fits.  

Key Alignments  

Align new connections  
 with who you actually are —  
 not who you are performing to be.  

When a new relationship begins,  
 notice honestly how you feel  
 in that person’s presence.  

Do you feel more like yourself,  
 or less?  

Are you at ease,  
 or constantly adjusting  
 to be liked?  

Alignment here means  
 moving toward people  
 who make it easy to be yourself —  
 and being thoughtful  
 about those who don’t.  

Align your existing relationships  
with who you have both become.  

Most relationships  
do not fail because of one moment.  

They drift out of alignment  
slowly —  
as each person changes  
without the relationship  
changing with them.  

Notice where  
the way you used to connect  
no longer fits.  

Have small conversations.  
Name what has changed.  
Ask what they need now.  
Share what you need now.  

Relationships realign  
through honesty —  
not through time alone.  

Align your response  
with the state you are in.  

When you are restless,  
you may rush.  

When you are intense,  
you may push.  

When you are heavy,  
you may withdraw.  

Before responding,  
notice your state.  

If you are not ready,  
pause.  

A short pause  
often brings you back  
into alignment —  
with the conversation,  
and with the person  
you want to be.  

Align your expectations  
with how the other person is wired.  

Most friction  
comes from expecting others  
to operate the way you do.  

One person needs space.  
Another needs to talk.  

One shows care through action.  
Another through words.  

Neither is wrong.  

Alignment means  
adjusting how you relate —  
to fit who they are,  
not who you expect them to be.  

Align with reality —  
not with resistance.  

Not every person  
will meet you as you hoped.  

Not every situation  
will unfold as you imagined.  

Not every relationship  
will understand you  
or grow with you.  

When this happens,  
there is a quiet choice.  

You can resist —  
replaying, judging, defending,  
building walls of bitterness.  

Or you can align  
with what is —  
acknowledging reality  
without needing it  
to be different.  

Alignment does not mean  
it doesn’t hurt.  

It does not mean  
agreeing  
or excusing.  

It means no longer  
fighting reality  
as it already is.  

Because resistance  
does not change  
what has happened.  

It only keeps you  
stuck inside it.  

People will fall short.  
 Situations will disappoint.  

You may not always understand why.  

Bitterness grows  
when we insist  
life should have been different.  

Acceptance grows  
when we allow it  
to be what it is —  
and move forward  
without carrying resentment.  

This is alignment  
at its deepest.  

Align with the truth  
when a relationship  
has run its course.  

Some relationships end.  
A friendship drifts.  
A partnership stops growing.  
A marriage reaches its limit.  

These are among  
the hardest moments  
a person can face.  

Alignment here  
does not mean rushing forward.  

It means being honest.  

Allowing the grief.  
Allowing the process.  

And returning, gently,  
to what supports you —  
the breath,  
reflection,  
writing,  
and the people  
still walking with you.  

You move through it  
by staying aligned  
with where you are —  
one honest day at a time.  

In time,  
a new shape of life emerges.  

Not the same as before.  
But one where you can feel  
steady,  
whole,  
and open again.  

Closing Reflection on Relationships  

Relationships are not static.  
Neither are you.  

Alignment is the practice  
of letting connection evolve —  
with honesty,  
presence,  
and care.  

When connections align,  
nurture them.  

When they don’t,  
letting go with care  
is also alignment.  

And when understanding  
is not possible —  
accepting without resistance  
is its own form of peace.  

Three Keys for Relationships  

Be • Adjust • Accept  

Be yourself —  
not who you think you should be.  

Adjust with care —  
to others and to the moment.  

Accept what cannot be changed —  
without resistance or bitterness.  

Closing  

Bringing the Three Together  

Health, work, and relationships  

are not separate compartments  

of your life.  

They flow into one another.  

When your body and mind  

are supported,  

you show up with more  

in your work  

and in your relationships.  

When your work  

fits who you are,  

it leaves you  

with energy  

for what matters most —  

your people,  

your rest,  

your own well-being.  

When your relationships  

are honest and aligned,  

they nourish you  

in ways that strengthen  

everything else.  

Alignment in one pillar  

quietly supports  

alignment in the others.  

And misalignment in one  

quietly drains  

the others.  

This is why  

we do not need  

to perfect anything  

all at once.  

A small shift in health  

may soften  

a tension at work.  

A small adjustment at work  

may bring more presence  

to a conversation with someone  

you love.  

A more honest relationship  

may give you the courage  

to listen more carefully  

to what your body and mind  

are asking of you.  

Begin wherever  

you feel the most friction.  

Make one small,  

conscious adjustment.  

Trust that the effect  

will ripple —  

quietly,  

steadily,  

across the whole  

of your life.  

Because in the end,  

alignment is not  

about managing three things.  

It is about living  

as one integrated person —  

whose body and mind,  

whose work,  

and whose relationships  

move together,  

in their own way,  

at each phase of life.
