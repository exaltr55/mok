# Practice 7 — I M Aligning

PART A — Learning the Practice

Through the practices you have explored so far,  
you have been returning —  
again and again —  

to Awareness,  
the space or silence in which all things arise  
and eventually dissolves,  
like waves in the ocean.  

With the breath.  
With the mind.  

With your inner dialogue.  
With your body.  

With your habits.  
Each practice offered a simple invitation —  
to pause, to notice,  
and to create a little more space in how you live.  

Now, in this final practice,  
I M Aligning, we bring everything together.  
Alignment is about living in greater harmony —  

with who you are,  
with how life is unfolding around you,  
and with what each phase of work  
and life is asking of you right now.  

It is not about becoming someone different.  
It is not about adding more to your plate.  

It is about understanding yourself more clearly —  
and making small,  
conscious choices that support  
that understanding.  

To understand yourself more clearly,  
it helps to begin with a simple recognition —  
each of us is born with a particular nature.  

A set of tendencies, rhythms,  
and ways of operating  
that are uniquely our own.  

We see this everywhere,  
once we start to look.  

For example,  
some people do their sharpest thinking in the early morning.  
Others come alive mid-afternoon.  

Some work best in long,  
uninterrupted stretches.  
Others in short, focused sprints.  

Some recover through solitude.  
Others through conversation.  

None of these is better or worse.  
They are simply different —  
expressions of the particular way each of us is made.  

And consistently working against your own nature has a cost  
that accumulates quietly over time.  
Rather than labelling yourself a fixed type,  
simply begin to observe.  

At different times, you may notice:  
A sense of restlessness —  

scattered, too many inputs.  
A sense of intensity —  
high drive, running close to the edge.  

A sense of heaviness —  
energy low, motivation harder to find.  
These are not problems.  

They are signals —  

your system communicating what is present,  
and what might need a little adjustment.  
The practice of alignment begins with learning to read these signals in yourself —  
and responding with care, rather than force.  

In the previous practice,  
you learned how to pause —  

to step out of automatic patterns  
and create space.  
That space is where alignment begins.  

In this practice,  
you begin to use  
that space to make more conscious choices —  

choices that are better suited to your nature,  
your current energy,  
and the life you are actually living.  

Not perfect choices.  
Just more aligned ones.  

When you begin to live this way,  
things shift —  
quietly but meaningfully.  
Effort becomes more effective.  

Work feels less like friction,  
and more like flow.  
Relationships feel less strained.  

Life does not become perfect.  
It begins to feel more coherent,  
more steady, and more easeful.  

Alignment is not control.  
It is not self-improvement in the conventional sense.  

It is responsiveness —  

your growing ability to notice what is present,  
recognize what is changing,  
and adjust with Awareness and care,  
again and again.  

In the next section,  
we bring this into your daily life —  
not as a concept to hold,  
but as something you can begin to live.  

PART B — Practicing I M Aligning

Alright — this is where everything starts to come together.  

Alignment isn't an idea to study.  
It's a small adjustment you make,  
again and again, across your day.  

No grand gestures required.  
Small, conscious shifts. Done often. Done gently.  

Throughout your day, something is always *active* in you —  
some flavor of energy that's shaping how you feel and respond.  

Maybe restlessness. Maybe intensity. Maybe a quiet heaviness.  
None of these are wrong. They're just *what's true right now*.  

Alignment starts the moment you notice —  
and then nudge things, gently, toward balance.  

Three simple moves cover most of it:  

If things feel **rushed** — slow down.  
If things feel **intense** — soften.  
If things feel **heavy** — gently activate.  

Not forcefully. Just enough to come back toward steady.  
You're not fixing yourself. You're tuning the instrument.  

## A Simple Daily Rhythm

Alignment begins in small moments throughout the day.  
In the morning,  
pause briefly before the day begins.  

Notice your body.  
Notice your energy.  

Notice what feels dominant right now.  
From that, make one small adjustment —  
within what your day allows.  

During the day, pause briefly and ask:  
Am I in balance right now?  

From there —  
adjust your pace, shift your effort,  
or take a short pause.  

One small shift is enough.  

In the evening, begin to slow down.  
Reduce stimulation.  
Let your system settle.  

You may reflect gently:  
Did I support myself today?  
Did my choices feel aligned?  

Then let the day close without overthinking.  
These small moments, practiced consistently,  
quietly build your alignment over time.  

## The Three Pillars of Alignment

Alignment becomes real when it shows up in your daily life.  

Three areas matter most:  
your **health**, your **work**, your **relationships**.  

Your **health** — your physical and mental state.  
Your **work** — how you operate at the professional level.  
Your **relationships** — how you relate to other people.  

These are the pillars that carry your life.  
Everything else is built on top.  

In each one, you're working with patterns that are already part of you —  
your body, your way of operating, your way of relating.  

The work is to honor those patterns —  
while staying responsive to what *this season* of life is asking.  

## A quick word of care

Knowing your pattern is not a license to be ruled by it.  

The earlier practices — the breath, the mind, the voice,  
the writing, the movement, the pause — have been building one thing:  
**Awareness**.  

Awareness lets you notice a tendency without being swept away by it.  
See an impulse without acting on it.  
Recognize a pattern without using it as an excuse  
to stay in what's draining you.  

Without Awareness, your pattern runs you.  
With Awareness, you get to work *with* it —  
skillfully, consciously, without becoming its prisoner.  

So as we walk through these three pillars, hold this gently:  

Your pattern isn't an identity to defend.  
It isn't a reason to stay in what's exhausting or unhealthy.  
It's just a starting point — a truth about how you're made,  
to be met with attention.  

Alignment isn't justifying habits.  
Alignment is choosing — *consciously* —  
with a clearer sense of who you are,  
what you need, and what this moment is really asking of you.  

There are tools out there to help you understand your nature better.  
Use them as companions, not verdicts.  
They show you what supports you, what drains you,  
and what each phase of life wants from you.  

## Aligning Health

You're made in a particular way.  

At the physical level, your body has its own constitution —  
a natural blend of elements and tendencies that shape  
how you feel, how you digest, how you move, how you rest.  

Your mind has its own rhythms too — its own sensitivities,  
its own way of holding or releasing what life brings.  

Together, body and mind form a living system that's uniquely yours.  

Some bodies run warm. Others run cool.  
Some minds move fast. Others go deep and slow.  
Some people digest in a flash. Others take their time.  
Some process emotion out loud. Others by going quiet.  
Some come alive in movement. Others in stillness.  
Some thrive on variety. Others on rhythm.  

These aren't habits you picked.  
They're built in.  

This is why no single routine works for everyone.  
What energizes one person can exhaust the next.  
What nourishes one body can unsettle another.  

Alignment in health starts with honesty:  
your body and mind have their *own* nature.  
Their own signals. Their own needs.  
Your job is to listen — not to copy someone else's playbook.  

Your body and mind are also not the same from one day to the next.  
Some days they are energized.  

Some days depleted.  
Some days restless.  

Some days slow.  
Your age changes them.  

Your stress changes them.  
The season changes them.  

The phase of life you are in changes them.  
Alignment in health is not about following the right routine.  

It is about meeting yourself where you actually are —  
today, not yesterday,  
not the version of you from ten years ago.  

It is a continuous conversation between the nature you were born with,  
and what your body  
and mind are asking of you now.  

### Key Alignments

Align with your mental state, not against it.  
Your mental state shapes how you experience everything.  

At times, the mind can feel busy,  
restless, or overwhelmed.  

At other times, it may feel low —  
due to a life situation, changes in the body,  
or simply from being overextended.  

This is part of being human.  
Alignment here is not about forcing the mind to feel better.  

It is about supporting it —  
with Awareness and simple actions.  
You can begin with small, consistent steps:  
Pause and take a few steady breaths.  

Create short breaks between activities.  
Limit constant input from screens and noise.  

Give yourself time to rest and reset.  
Write or speak what you are processing,  
instead of holding it in.  

Reach out to your cohort —  
the others walking this path alongside you.  
Sometimes simply being heard by people who understand the practices you are living is enough to shift something  
that felt stuck.  

These are simple shifts —  
but they change how the mind moves.  
Over time, this creates more space,  
more clarity, and more steadiness.  

So the mind does not drain you —  
it begins to support you.  

Align what you do with the body  
and mind you have today,  
not the ones you used to have.  

The exercise that served you at twenty  
or thirty may not serve you at forty or fifty.  
The late nights you once recovered from easily may now cost you three days.  

The foods that once energized you may now feel heavy.  
And the way your mind used to bounce back quickly may now ask for more quiet,  
more space, more recovery.  

Notice this honestly, without resistance.  
Your body and mind have not failed you.  

They have changed.  
Alignment means adjusting what you do to match who you are now —  
not pushing to keep up with who you were.  

Align your rhythm with what your system actually needs.  
Some people feel their best  
when life follows a predictable rhythm.  

Meals at the same time.  
Sleep at the same hour.  

A steady routine that the body  
and mind can rely on.  
For them, consistency is not rigidity —  
it is what keeps their system stable.  

Other people operate differently.  
They can work for long stretches without a set schedule,  
eat when it fits, sleep when the day allows,  
and still feel steady.  

Their system does not need the same structure to function well.  
Some fall in the middle of the spectrum.  

No way is better.  
What matters is noticing which kind of system you actually have —  
and honoring it.  

If you are someone who needs consistency,  
protect your rhythms.  
Do not let a busy season quietly dismantle them.  

If you are someone who thrives with flexibility,  
allow that —  

without forcing yourself into routines  
that do not fit how you operate.  

Alignment here is simply choosing a rhythm  
that matches your system,  
not one you think you should follow.  

Align your pace with the energy actually available to you.  
Check in, honestly,  
at different points in the day.  

Not just physical energy —  
but mental energy too.  
If you are clear and focused,  
use that clarity for what matters most.  

If your energy is moderate,  
take on what is steady and manageable.  
If your energy is low in body or mind —  
do not pretend otherwise.  

Rest if you can, or shift, or simplify.  
Fighting against the energy you have wastes more than it gains.  

Working with it carries you further.  
Align your food with what the moment needs.  

Food is not just fuel.  
It shapes how you feel, how you think,  
and how you show up for the next few hours.  

It affects your body  
and your mind in the same meal.  
Before you eat, pause and ask:  
What do I need right now?  

Lightness, so I can stay clear?  
Grounding, so I can steady myself?  

Warmth, so I can settle?  
Let your choice match the answer —  
not habit, not convenience,  
not what someone else is having.  

Align your recovery with the demands you are carrying.  
Not all days are the same.  

A week of heavy work asks for more recovery, not less.  
A period of travel, stress,  
or change asks for more rest,  
not pushing through.  

A time of emotional difficulty asks for gentleness —  
not willpower.  

Notice what you are actually carrying,  
in body and mind —  
and give yourself the recovery  
that matches it.  

This is not indulgence.  
It is alignment between what life is asking of you  
and what you give yourself to sustain it.  

### Closing Reflection on Health

Your body and mind are your closest companions through life.  
They change.  

You change.  
Alignment in health is not a fixed formula.  

It is a practice of meeting yourself honestly —  
noticing what you have, what you need,  
and choosing accordingly.  

When you do this consistently,  
your body and mind repay you —  
not with perfection, but with steadiness.  

### Three Keys for Health

**Know • Honor • Adjust**

Know your body and mind, and what they need.  
Honor the habits that genuinely fit you.  
Adjust gently as your body, your season,  
and your life continue to change.  

## Aligning Work

Work is where many of us spend most of our time  
and energy.  
When your work fits who you are,  
effort feels meaningful.  

When it doesn’t, even small tasks feel heavy.  
Alignment in work is the ongoing practice of closing the gap between who you are  
and what you do.  

Sometimes this means choosing differently  
when you can.  
Sometimes it means shaping the work you already have to better fit your nature.  

Both are acts of alignment.  
Some people are natural builders —  
they thrive in creating,  
in taking an idea from nothing to something.  

Some are natural organizers —  
they bring order, structure,  
and steadiness to what already exists.  

Some are natural connectors —  
they work best through people,  
relationships, and collaboration.  

Some are natural thinkers —  
drawn to ideas, analysis, and depth.  
Some are natural doers —  
energized by action, execution, and momentum.  

These are not roles you learned.  
They are inclinations you were born with —  

a living pattern  
that shapes how you engage with work,  
what energizes you,  
and where your contribution feels most natural.  

### Key Alignments

Align your work choices with how you actually operate —  
not how you think you should operate.  

Before taking on a new role,  
a project, or a major commitment —  

pause, and take yourself through a few honest questions:  
Does this work use the strengths  
that come naturally to me?  

Does it match the pace I operate at best?  
Does it suit the phase of life I am in right now?

Will I be able to sustain this over time,  
without losing myself in the process?  

You can also take simple, practical steps:  
write out what energizes you  
and what drains you,  
based on the last few months of your work.  

Before accepting something new,  
sit with it for at least a day —  
not respond in the moment.  

Talk to someone who knows both you  
and the opportunity,  
and listen carefully to what they notice.  

You will not always have a full choice.  

But when you do,  
choose in a way  
that fits the person you actually are —  
not the person you are trying to prove yourself to be.  

Align the work you already have with the parts of you  
that do it best.  
Most of us are not starting fresh.  

We are already inside a role,  
a set of responsibilities, a body of work.  
Alignment here is not about walking away.  

It is about reshaping what you already have —  
slowly, steadily, and realistically.  

You can begin with small,  
practical moves:  
Take one week to notice which parts of your work energize you,  
and which parts drain you.  

Make a simple list —  

energizing on one side,  
draining on the other.  
Pick one draining task —  
and ask honestly: Can I delegate this?  

Can I simplify this?  
Can I renegotiate this with my manager,  
my team, or my client?  

Pick one energizing task —  
and ask: Can I do more of this?  
Can I lean into it when I plan my week?  

Block a portion of your calendar for the work you do best,  
before it gets filled by everything else.  
Alignment here is rarely dramatic.  

It is small,  
steady adjustments that, over time,  
reshape your work to fit you more closely.  
Align your hardest work with your sharpest self.  

You do not bring the same clarity to every hour of the day.  
Your mind has peaks, and it has valleys —  
and working with them makes a real difference.  

You can begin by getting to know your own rhythm:  
For one week,  
notice when your mind feels sharpest.  
Often, it is one to three hours in the morning.  

Sometimes, mid-afternoon.  
For some, it is late evening  
when the world is quiet.  

Once you know your window,  
protect it with intention:  

Schedule your most demanding  
or creative work for that window.  
Do not give it to routine email,  
low-priority meetings, or small tasks.  

Turn off notifications during this window  
when you can.  

Save meetings, correspondence,  
and execution tasks for the steadier  
but less sharp hours.  

When your energy dips, do the simpler work —  
or take a genuine break.  
This one alignment, practiced consistently,  
often creates more real progress than working longer hours ever will.  

Align your work with the phase of life you are in.  
The work that served you at one stage of life may not serve you at another.  

In your twenties,  
you may have had energy to spare.  
In your forties,  
you may be carrying more responsibility at home.  

In your fifties or sixties,  
you may be looking for meaning over intensity.  
Each phase asks different things of you.  

Alignment here is about noticing what this phase needs —  
and responding with care, not guilt.  
You can do this with a few simple practices:  

Once every few months,  
set aside an honest hour to ask yourself:  
What is working?  

What is no longer working?  
What does this phase of my life need  
that I am not giving it?  

Notice where your time is going —  
and whether it matches what actually matters to you right now.  
If something feels off, start small.  

Adjust one commitment.  
Let go of one obligation that no longer fits.  

Add one thing  
that supports who you are becoming.  
You do not need to change everything at once.  

But you do deserve work  
that honors the stage you are actually in.  

### Closing Reflection on Work

Work will not always be easy.  
But when it fits who you are —  
at this phase of your life — it carries you.  

When it doesn't, it wears you down,  
quietly, over time.  

Alignment in work is the practice of closing  
that gap —  
not all at once, but steadily.  

Small adjustments, made with Awareness,  
that bring your daily work closer  
and closer to the person you actually are.  

### Three Keys for Work

**Choose • Shape • Evolve**

Choose work that fits who you are,  
when you have the choice.  

Shape the work already in your hands around your strengths  
and your rhythms.  
Evolve your work as you move through the different phases of your life.  

## Aligning Relationships

Relationships are one of the deepest sources of meaning in our lives.  
They are also one of the most common sources of misalignment.  

We change.  
The other person changes.  

Life changes around both of us.  
But the shape of our relationships often stays the same —  
long after it has stopped fitting who we have become.  

Alignment in relationships is the practice of noticing those shifts,  
and adjusting the connection —  
so that it continues to support who you are now,  
and who the other person is now.  

Sometimes this means forming new connections.  
Sometimes deepening  
or renegotiating existing ones.  

And sometimes,  
gently releasing what no longer fits.  

### Key Alignments

Align new connections with who you actually are —  
not who you are performing to be.  

When a new relationship begins,  
notice honestly how you feel in  
that person’s presence.  

Do you feel more like yourself, or less?  
Are you at ease,  
or constantly adjusting to be liked?  

Alignment here means moving toward people who make it easy to be yourself —  
and being thoughtful about those who don’t.  
Align your existing relationships with who you have both become.  

Most relationships do not fail  
because of one moment.  
They drift out of alignment slowly —  
as each person changes without the relationship changing with them.  

Notice where the way you used to connect no longer fits.  
Have small conversations.  

Name what has changed.  
Ask what they need now.  

Share what you need now.  
Relationships realign through honesty —  
not through time alone.  

Align your response with the state you are in.  
When you are restless, you may rush.  

When you are intense, you may push.  
When you are heavy, you may withdraw.  

Before responding, notice your state.  
If you are not ready, pause.  

A short pause often brings you back into alignment —  
with the conversation,  
and with the person you want to be.  
Align your expectations with how the other person is wired.  

Most friction comes from expecting others to operate the way you do.  
One person needs space.  

Another needs to talk.  
One shows care through action.  

Another through words.  
Neither is wrong.  

Alignment means adjusting how you relate —  
to fit who they are,  
not who you expect them to be.  
Align with reality — not with resistance.  

Not every person will meet you as you hoped.  
Not every situation will unfold as you imagined.  

Not every relationship will understand you  
or grow with you.  
When this happens, there is a quiet choice.  

You can resist —  

replaying, judging, defending,  
building walls of bitterness.  
Or you can align with what is —  
acknowledging reality without needing it to be different.  

Alignment does not mean it doesn’t hurt.  
It does not mean agreeing or excusing.  

It means no longer fighting reality as it already is.  
Because resistance does not change what has happened.  

It only keeps you stuck inside it.  

People will fall short.  
Situations will disappoint.  
You may not always understand why.  

Bitterness grows  
when we insist life should have been different.  

Acceptance grows  
when we allow it to be what it is —  
and move forward without carrying resentment.  

This is alignment at its deepest.  
Align with the truth  
when a relationship has run its course.  

Some relationships end.  
A friendship drifts.  

A partnership stops growing.  
A marriage reaches its limit.  

These are among the hardest moments a person can face.  
Alignment here does not mean rushing forward.  

It means being honest.  
Allowing the grief.  

Allowing the process.  
And returning, gently, to what supports you —  
the breath, reflection, writing,  
and the people still walking with you.  

You move through it by staying aligned with where you are —  
one honest day at a time.  
In time, a new shape of life emerges.  

Not the same as before.  
But one where you can feel steady,  
whole, and open again.  

### Closing Reflection on Relationships

Relationships are not static.  
Neither are you.  

Alignment is the practice of letting connection evolve —  
with honesty, presence, and care.  
When connections align, nurture them.  

When they don’t,  
letting go with care is also alignment.  
And when understanding is not possible —  
accepting without resistance is its own form of peace.  

### Three Keys for Relationships

**Be • Adjust • Accept**

Be yourself —  
not who you think you should be.  

Adjust with care —  
to others and to the moment.  

Accept what cannot be changed —  
without resistance or bitterness.  

## Bringing the Three Together

Health, work,  
and relationships are not separate compartments of your life.  
They flow into one another.  

When your body and mind are supported,  
you show up with more in your work  
and in your relationships.  

When your work fits who you are,  
it leaves you with energy for what matters most —  
your people, your rest, your own well-being.  

When your relationships are honest  
and aligned,  
they nourish you in ways  
that strengthen everything else.  

Alignment in one pillar quietly supports alignment in the others.  

And misalignment in one quietly drains the others.  
This is why we do not need to perfect anything all at once.  

A small shift in health may soften a tension at work.  
A small adjustment at work may bring more presence to a conversation with someone you love.  

A more honest relationship may give you the courage to listen more carefully to what your body  
and mind are asking of you.  
Begin wherever you feel the most friction.  

Make one small, conscious adjustment.  
Trust that the effect will ripple —  
quietly, steadily,  
across the whole of your life.  

Because in the end,  
alignment is not about managing three things.  
It is about living as one integrated person —  

whose body and mind, whose work,  
and whose relationships move together,  
in their own way, at each phase of life.  

## Your alignment practice in action

Here's the practical heart of I M Aligning —  
a clear action plan at four cadences.  

Each one walks through the same three pillars —  
**Health, Work, Relationships** —  
but with different depth and frequency.  

Start with daily. Add the rest as your rhythm settles.  

### Daily — 2 to 3 minutes

Once in the day — pick a time you'll actually keep.  
Morning is ideal. Evening works too.  

Take one breath. Then a quiet scan of all three:  

**Body.**  
Energy: 1 to 5?  
What does it need right now —  
food, water, movement, rest?  

**Work.**  
Am I working from my strengths today,  
or pushing against the grain?  
What's the one task that actually matters?  

**Relationships.**  
Who am I going to show up for today?  
Anyone I owe a message, a call,  
or a real conversation?  

Then make **one small shift**.  
Drink the water. Tackle the right task first.  
Send the message.  

That's it. Two or three minutes.  
The point isn't to fix everything —  
it's to notice, and nudge.  

### Weekly — 10 to 15 minutes

Once a week. Sunday evening or Saturday morning work well.  
A quiet seat, a journal, something warm to drink.  

Walk through each pillar and rate it 1 to 5:  

**Health this week.**  
Did my body get the food, sleep, and movement it needed?  
Did my mind get quiet time?  
What drained me? What energized me?  
**Rate: 1 to 5.**  

**Work this week.**  
Did I spend my hours on what matters most,  
or on what was loudest?  
What did I lean into? What pulled me off-course?  
**Rate: 1 to 5.**  

**Relationships this week.**  
Who showed up for me? Who did I show up for?  
Anything left unsaid? Any tension building?  
**Rate: 1 to 5.**  

The lowest score is your area to fine-tune.  

Pick **one specific shift** for next week.  
Not "exercise more" — *"a 20-minute walk on Mon, Wed, Fri."*  
Not "communicate better" — *"call Mom this Tuesday evening."*  
Concrete. Doable. One thing.  

### Monthly — 30 to 45 minutes

Once a month — end of the month,  
or the first of the new one.  

Pull up your four weekly check-ins  
and look for the pattern.  

**Trends.**  
Which pillar has been consistently lowest?  
Which has been quietly draining the others?  
Where did the small weekly shifts actually stick —  
and where did they fizzle?  

**One bigger move.**  
What's the one **bigger adjustment** this month is asking for?  
A boundary at work.  
A routine you've been avoiding.  
A conversation you've been postponing.  
A standing date you want to put on the calendar.  

**Plan it.**  
Pick the move. Name the date.  
Tell someone if accountability helps.  

You're not solving everything.  
You're naming the one thing this month wants.  

### Seasonal — Every 3 months, 1 to 2 hours

Four times a year — every 3 months —  
take a longer look.  

Pick a date and protect it.  
End of a quarter. A change of season.  
Somewhere quiet. Phone off. Notebook open.  

This is where the bigger questions live —  
the ones daily and weekly check-ins are too small to hold.  

**Health.**  
Has my body changed this season? My energy?  
My sleep? My weight?  
Is the way I take care of myself  
still serving who I am right now?  

**Work.**  
Is what I do still matching who I am,  
in this chapter of my life?  
Where is the friction? Where is the flow?  
What no longer fits — and what do I need more of?  

**Relationships.**  
Where am I deeply connected? Where have I drifted?  
Who do I need to reach out to?  
What relationship needs honest attention this quarter?  

Close with one honest question:  
*What would the next three months look like  
if I were more aligned?*  

Pick one or two answers. Live into them.  
The rest can wait until the next season.  

### A simple reminder

You don't need to be perfect at all four scales.  

One daily check-in,  
practiced consistently for a month,  
will quietly shift more in your life  
than any elaborate yearly plan that never lands.  

Begin with daily.  
Let the weekly join you when it feels natural.  
Monthly and seasonal will arrive when they're ready.  

The whole system is here to serve you —  
not the other way around.
