# Practice 3 — I M Talking

In the previous practice,  
we used a simple anchor thought  
to notice the space between thoughts—  
the space of Awareness, the Source.  

As we begin to notice thoughts more clearly,  
another pattern becomes visible.  

Not all thoughts are new.  
Some repeat.  
Some return again and again.  

Over time,  
these repeated thoughts  
begin to feel like a conversation—  
a conversation we are having with ourselves.  

This is what we call self-talk.  

Let’s face it—  
we all talk to ourselves.  
Throughout the day.  
Often without realizing it.  

Sometimes the inner voice is supportive.  
Sometimes it is critical.  

Often, it simply repeats old patterns—  
shaped by past experiences,  
conditioning, and memory.  

The question is not  
whether we talk to ourselves.  

The question is:  
how consciously we do it—  

and whether the way we speak to ourselves  
is serving us  
or holding us back.  

In the previous practice, I M Thinking,  
we learned to observe thoughts.  

Here, we take the next step.  

If we can notice thoughts,  
we are no longer limited  
to repeating the same ones.  

We can begin to choose them.  

This practice of consciously shaping thoughts  
is called I M Talking.  

It is about learning to speak to yourself  
from Awareness—  
with intention, care, and clarity.  

The voice within matters.  
We hear it more than any other voice.  

When that voice is harsh or doubtful,  
it drains our energy.  

When it is steady and supportive,  
it becomes an ally.  

This inner dialogue shapes  
how we feel,  
what we believe,  
and how we respond to life.  

Once self-talk is brought into Awareness,  
a simple but powerful shift becomes possible:  
we can guide it.  

For this practice, we use affirmations.  

Affirmations are short,  
intentional “I am” statements.  

They are not wishes.  
They are not promises for the future.  
They are ways of speaking to yourself  
in the present moment.  

Think of affirmations  
like planting seeds.  

A seed does not grow overnight.  
It is repeated,  
nurtured,  
given attention.  

Over time,  
it takes root.  

In the same way,  
repeated thoughts shape beliefs.  
Beliefs shape behavior.  
And behavior shapes experience.  

Core Affirmations  

Here are the core affirmations  
you can begin with:  

I am caring for my health,  
in body and mind.  

I am welcoming abundance,  
as it flows to me.  

I am noticing what I'm grateful for,  
appreciating what is here.  

I am expressing my gifts and talents,  
in service of others.  

I am staying open,  
trusting how life unfolds.  

I am giving my full attention,  
to what is in front of me.  

I am choosing freely,  
in ways that honor everyone.  

I am.  

Adding Your Own  

In addition to the seven core affirmations,  
you can also create your own affirmations  
based on what you need in the moment.  

For example, during a difficult time:  

I am allowing myself to grieve.  
I am holding myself with compassion.  
I am finding strength, one moment at a time.  

Simple Guidelines  

When creating affirmations:  

Keep them in the present tense  
 —not “I will be,” but “I am.”  

Begin with “I am.”  
This anchors the statement  
in Awareness—  
in the Source you are.  

When to Use This Practice  

You can use I M Talking  
right after I M Thinking,  
when the mind is quieter  
and more receptive.  

Or at any moment during the day—  
between meetings,  
before a conversation,  
or whenever you pause.  

You don’t need to go through  
a full set of affirmations.  

Even one simple statement  
is enough.  

For example,  
while taking a shower,  
you might gently say to yourself:  

I am caring for my health,  
in body and mind.  

In small moments like these,  
the practice becomes part of daily life.  

Even a few moments  
of conscious self-talk  
can shift how you experience the moment.  

That brings us gently  
to the next practice.  

PART B — Daily Practice  

Here we go.  

Find a comfortable seat,  
or just pause wherever you are.  

(Just not while driving, operating machinery,  
or doing anything that needs your full attention —  
the inner voice gets a turn now.)  

Take one or two slow breaths.  
Let the body settle.  
Let your shoulders drop  
(yes, they're holding on to something — let them).  

Now we'll speak to ourselves — gently, deliberately —  
seven simple lines.  
Let each one land.  
Don't rush.  

*I am caring for my health,*  
*in body and mind.*  

*I am welcoming abundance,*  
*as it flows to me.*  

*I am noticing what I'm grateful for,*  
*appreciating what is here.*  

*I am expressing my gifts and talents,*  
*in service of others.*  

*I am staying open,*  
*trusting how life unfolds.*  

*I am giving my full attention,*  
*to what is in front of me.*  

*I am choosing freely,*  
*in ways that honor everyone.*  

And finally —  

*I am.*  

Let the words soften.  
Let them fade.  

Rest there for a moment.  
Notice what's left when the words go quiet.  

Then ease your way back  
into your day.  

Carry the *I am* with you.  
It'll be useful.
