# Practice 4 — I M Writing

PART A — Learning the Practice  

After reshaping how we speak to ourselves through I M Talking,  
we now give our inner voice a place to land.  

On paper.  

Writing is one of the simplest ways  
to slow the mind down  
and see ourselves more clearly.  

Thoughts moving in the mind  
can feel crowded and tangled.  

But when we write them down,  
they line up—  
one by one—  
waiting to be seen.  

Writing creates space.  
And in that space,  
clarity begins to appear.  

This practice is called I M Writing.  

It is about translating  
your inner world into words.  

Not to document your day,  
but to understand it.  

Not to judge yourself,  
but to listen.  

When we write:  

What felt vague  
becomes clearer.  

What felt heavy  
becomes lighter.  

What felt fleeting  
often turns into insight.  

Writing can take many forms.  

You might write about your day.  
You might explore an emotion or challenge.  

You might reflect on a decision.  
You might list what you’re grateful for.  
You might capture an insight or idea.  

All of these are welcome.  

In this practice,  
each day we can use one of three simple styles of writing:  

Expressive,  
Reflective,  
Gratitude.  

**Expressive Writing**  

This style is about releasing emotions  
and clearing mental clutter.  

You write freely,  
without editing or censoring.  

Just letting what’s inside  
move onto the page.  

**Reflective Writing**  

This style is about learning from experience.  

You look back at events  
and ask what they are teaching you.  

You begin to notice patterns,  
choices, and growth.  

**Gratitude Writing**  

This style is about noticing what's working.  

Small things.  
Ordinary things.  

Kind acts of People,  
Moments that might otherwise be missed.  

Over time,  
this gently trains the mind  
to see more balance and goodness.  

Unlike conversations with others,  
writing offers a judgment-free space.  

The page doesn’t interrupt.  
It doesn’t argue.  
It doesn’t correct you.  

It simply listens.  

And in that quiet listening,  
your authentic voice emerges.  

Why does this matter?  

Because writing slows the mind  
in a way very few things do.  

When thoughts stay in the head,  
they tend to loop.  

When they land on the page,  
they settle.  

Once written,  
we can see our thoughts,  
reflect on them,  
and often gain a new perspective.  

This practice returns us to Awareness —  
making us conscious  
of what's going on  
at the mental level.  

People who practice I M Writing  
often notice:  

Greater self-awareness.  
Less emotional overload.  
More clarity and perspective.  
A growing sense of gratitude and balance.  

Not because life becomes simpler—  
but because the inner world  
becomes more organized and spacious.  

When to Use This Practice  

I M Writing works especially well  
at the end of the day.  

Before sleeping.  
When things begin to slow down.  

It can also be used  
after I M Talking,  
to give your affirmations, emotions,  
and reflections a place to settle.  

Even five minutes daily  
is enough to make a difference.  

A Simple Habit Tip  

To make this practice easier to return to,  
keep your journal next to your bed.  

When it’s within reach,  
there’s no friction.  

At the end of the day,  
you can simply pick it up,  

write a few lines,  
and let the day settle  
before you sleep.  

Small placement choices like this  
often make the biggest difference  
in building a steady practice.  

Now, let’s walk through  
how to practice this.  

PART B — Daily Practice  

First, pick your notebook of choice —  
a real one, a journal, or a digital space.  
Whatever you'll actually open tomorrow.  

Find a quiet moment.  
Bedtime works beautifully —  
the page is good at holding what sleep doesn't need to.  

Before you start, take a breath  
and ask yourself a small question:  

*What does today need?*  

Release? Reflection? Gratitude?  
(One of the three is usually obvious. Pick it.)  

**If it's release —**  
write freely. Let it out.  
No filter, no editing in your head as you go.  
Just let it move.  

For example:  
*"I felt anxious before the meeting today,*  
*but once it started, I realized I was more prepared than I thought."*  

**If it's reflection —**  
look for the meaning, the learning, the pattern.  

For example:  
*"Today reminded me how much clear communication matters*  
*in my closest relationships."*  

**If it's gratitude —**  
list what you're thankful for. Specific is better than grand.  

For example:  
*"I'm grateful for the quiet walk this evening. It helped me reset."*  

Now write — continuously — for three to five minutes.  
Longer if you want. Shorter if that's what's true.  

Don't edit. Don't judge. Don't go back and fix anything.  
Spelling has no power here.  

When you're ready to close,  
end with one positive note —  
something you appreciate about yourself or someone in your life.  

For example:  
*"I'm thankful for the effort I showed today."*  

That's it. The day is set down.  
Sleep well.
