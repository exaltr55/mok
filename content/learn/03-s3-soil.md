# S3 — Soil

The third S stands for **Soil**.

We learned earlier  
that we are the awareness, the Source,

and we give rise to thoughts  
in order to experience.

A thought-pattern — the Seed —  
is like the script of a drama.

On its own,  
a script is only potential.

For a drama to come alive,  
it needs actors,  
a stage,  
and supporting conditions  
through which the story  
can be expressed.

In the same way,  
a Seed does not become experience  
by itself.

It requires real-world conditions  
to be lived.

These conditions appear as:

the situations we face,  
the people we meet  
and interact with,  
the roles and responsibilities  
we step into,  
and the circumstances  
that surround us.

When a Seed meets these conditions,  
the pattern begins to express itself  
as lived experience.

The matching conditions  
that allow a Seed to unfold  
are what we call **Soil**.

Soil is not a reward  
or a punishment.

It is not personal or selective.

It is resonance —

a natural alignment  
between a pattern  
and the conditions  
that allow it to take form in life.

Just as an apple seed  
grows only in soil  
that can support it,

our thought patterns meet conditions  
that allow them to be experienced.

Another way to see this  
is through the example  
of a mirror.

A mirror, like Soil,  
does not add its own character.

It simply reflects back  
what is placed in front of it.

Soil does not ask  
to be judged.

It asks to be seen.

Why do we care about Soil?

To recognize that the conditions  
in which our patterns are lived  
reflect the thought-patterns  
we have cultivated over time.

Soil is not random.

It does not judge  
or assign specific outcomes.

It simply mirrors the Seed  
we create.

By learning to observe Soil —  
as it shows up  
through people, situations,  
roles, responsibilities,  
and surrounding conditions —

we are learning to recognize  
our patterns,  
not as judgments,  
but as information.

When we see our patterns clearly —  
through awareness —

blame naturally softens.

We stop turning against ourselves  
and stop assigning fault to others.

This shift moves us  
from automatic reaction  
to conscious responsibility,

opening the space  
for choice, learning, and growth.

Consider what happens at work.

When similar situations,  
dynamics, or challenges  
repeat across projects,  
teams, or roles,

it is not a sign  
that something is wrong.

It is a sign  
that a pattern — a Seed —  
is being expressed  
through familiar conditions.

The same can be observed  
in relationships,  
especially in a marriage.

We may notice familiar reactions,  
expectations, or conflicts  
arising with different people  
and in different contexts.

The faces may change,  
but the experience feels familiar.

This does not mean  
the situation  
or the other person  
is the cause.

It means a Seed — a pattern —  
is being lived  
through the Soil  
that is available.

By understanding Soil  
and its relationship to Seed,

we stop asking,  
"Who is at fault?"

and begin asking,

"What pattern is unfolding,  
and through which conditions?"

That question is where  
conscious change becomes possible.

This understanding empowers us  
to take responsibility  
for our life experiences.

As we continue  
to understand our experiences,  
another truth becomes clear:

The Source remains constant.

Everything else —  
including the conditions  
through which experience unfolds —  
is changing.

Even the conditions — the Soil —  
through which a Seed expresses itself  
are not static.

They shift, evolve,  
and unfold over time.

As conditions change,  
the pattern continues  
to express itself  
through different phases  
and stages of our life journey.

To understand how  
this expression unfolds over time,

we turn to the next S:

**Seasons.**
