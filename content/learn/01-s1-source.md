# S1 — Source

Every experience you have —  
a thought, an emotion, a reaction,  
a moment of focus or stress —

arises within an inner space  
that is always present.

This space has been called  
many names:  
awareness, presence, silence,  
attention, or the observer.

In YouSourceful,  
we refer to this space  
as the **Source** —  
the first S of the five Ss.

The Source is not a belief  
or a philosophy.

It is the simple fact  
that you are aware.

You can confirm this  
right now.

Become aware of your breath.

Pause —  
and notice the next thought  
that arises.

Now ask yourself:

If you can notice the breath,  
are you the breath?

If you can notice the thought,  
are you the thought?

Anything you can be aware of  
cannot be who you are.

So what remains?

What remains  
is awareness itself —

the space in which the breath is felt  
and thoughts arise and pass.

We use the word **Source**  
not as an idea to be accepted  
or a concept to be debated,

but as a simple way of pointing  
to what is already present  
in your direct experience.

You are the Source —  
the awareness  
in which all experience arises  
and dissolves.

The Source does not come and go.  
It does not improve or decline.

It is present  
when the mind is calm  
and when it is busy.

It is present  
when the body is active  
and when it is at rest.

This awareness is not divided.

At the level of Source,  
there is no separation —  
no "your" Source  
or "my" Source.

There is one Source,  
expressing itself  
through many forms.

Each of us lives  
through different bodies,  
minds, histories,  
and conditions.

But the awareness  
in which experience happens  
is the same.

Like waves  
in a single ocean,

each wave appears separate —  
with its own form,  
movement,  
and moment.

But the ocean  
in which they arise  
and dissolve  
is one.

In the same way,  
awareness may appear  
as many —

but its Source  
is not divided.

In daily life,  
we tend to operate  
in two ways:

when the Source  
is unnoticed,  
and when the Source  
is recognized.

When the Source is **unnoticed**,  
we identify completely  
with our thoughts,  
emotions,  
and conditions —

experiencing them  
as who we are.

When the Source is **recognized**,  
experience continues —  
but is lived  
with more space,  
clarity,  
and perspective.

Thoughts, emotions,  
and life situations  
are still present,

but they are seen  
as experiences —  
not identity.

This recognition  
does not remove responsibility.

We continue to own  
our thoughts and actions.

What changes  
is how we relate  
to experience —

with more acceptance,  
less resistance,  
and greater clarity  
of choice.

Pain may still arise,  
but suffering reduces —

because experience  
is no longer happening  
*to* us,  
but *within* us.

Like waves  
in the ocean,  
they arise,  
remain for a time,  
and dissolve.

A natural question arises:

Where do we find the Source?

The Source is not something  
to search for or attain.

It is what you already are —  
the awareness  
in which all experience unfolds.

It is not attained,  
but recognized —  
and gradually lived from.

If the Source  
is not something to seek,  
how do we recognize it  
and live from it?

Through practice.

This is what we begin  
to develop  
in the second part  
of this system.

You will learn  
to notice your breath,  
to notice the space  
between thoughts —

and in that noticing,  
recognize directly  
your nature  
as the Source.

With this understanding —  
that you are the Awareness,  
and that all experience  
arises within it —

we can now turn  
to a deeper question:

How does experience  
take form within the Awareness?

This is where we move  
to the next S:

the **Seed.**
