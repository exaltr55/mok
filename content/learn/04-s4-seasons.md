# S4 — Season · Time, Unfolding, and Alignment

Here we meet Season —  
the cycles through which  
everything unfolds.

An apple seed  
does not become an apple tree  
overnight.

It unfolds over time —  

through sprouting,  
growth,  
flowering,  
and fruiting.

Some plants move through  
these stages quickly.

Others take much longer.

Our life experience  
unfolds in the same way.

The Seed we create —  
our thought pattern —  
meets Soil  
and expresses itself through time.

What we experience  
in the present moment  
is often part of a longer process  
already in motion.

We refer to each phase  
of an unfolding life experience  
as a Season.

A Season, therefore,  
is simply Soil unfolding  
through a cycle of time.

Each Season carries  
its own conditions,  
constraints,  
and possibilities.

One Season may support  
action and execution.

Another may call for understanding,  
integration, or patience.

A Season may bring expansion  
across different aspects of life,

or it may bring periods  
of restriction and consolidation.

Each Season serves its purpose —  
providing the conditions  
through which experience unfolds.

Why does recognizing the Season matter?

Recognizing the Season  
we are in helps us see  
where we are in the unfolding —

instead of responding  
as if every moment  
requires the same kind  
of effort or response.

In our work,  
this is easy to observe.

Most work naturally moves  
through cycles —

planning and sense-making,

focused action and execution,

review, learning,  
and course correction.

A project, for example,  
moves through design,  
development, deployment,  
review, and maintenance —

each phase calling on  
a different set of skills,  
different resources,  
different team members.

Each phase calls for  
a different quality of attention  
and a different kind of response.

Action taken without clarity  
creates friction.

When clarity is present,  
effort becomes effective  
and sustainable.

Seasonal understanding  
helps us distinguish  
between pressure-driven reaction  
and action that is timely.

It does not ask you to do less.  
It invites you to act  
more intelligently —

in alignment with timing,  
context,  
and what is unfolding.

Pause and notice:

What is the timing here?

What is the context?

What is this moment  
actually calling for?

When action is aligned  
in this way,  
decisions become clearer  
and progress becomes more natural.

Consider the weather season  
of winter.

Winter is not a problem  
to be solved  
or a failure of summer  
to continue.

It is a different set of conditions.

When winter arrives,  
we naturally adjust.

We change what we eat —  
choosing warmer,  
grounding foods.

We sleep a little longer  
and allow for more rest.

We modify how we exercise,  
favoring slower,  
gentler movement.

We often turn inward,  
spending more time  
in reflection,

and more time in connection —  
gathering socially  
in warmer,  
quieter ways.

We do not expect  
the same rhythms from winter  
that we expect from summer.

We align with  
what the season calls for.

In the same way,  
each Season of our inner life — the unfolding Soil —  
calls for its own response,  
needs adjustment for alignment.

Progress does not always look  
like constant action.

Sometimes it looks like rest,  
consolidation,  
or recalibration.

When we stop fighting  
the conditions of the Season  
we are in,  
and begin responding to it  
intelligently,

our effort becomes  
more effective,  
sustainable,  
and steadying.

Season, as we learned,  
is where we experience  
what has already been  
set in motion.

Once this is understood,  
a natural and empowering question  
arises:

If I am the creator  
of my life experiences,  
how can I create those experiences  
consciously?

That question brings us  
to the next S:

Sowing.
